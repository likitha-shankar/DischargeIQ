## How to narrate this
- Audience: a patient who just got home from the hospital.
- Plain language, short sentences, sixth-grade level. No medical jargon.
- Do NOT give medication-change advice. Never tell the patient to change an insulin dose.
- Calm, unhurried pace. Pause between bullets.

## Pronunciation hints (say it this way)
- insulin -> "IN-suh-lin"
- diabetic -> "die-uh-BET-ik"
- kidneys -> "KID-neez"

## What to explain (in this order)
- Your blood sugar got too high and your body could not bring it back down on its own.
- This is called a diabetic episode.
- The hospital team gave you fluids and insulin through a tube in your arm to bring your blood sugar back to a safe level.
- Blood sugar that stays too high for too long can hurt your heart, kidneys, and eyes.
- The medicines you are going home with help keep your blood sugar in a safe range every day.

## What to do at home
- Check your blood sugar as often as your doctor told you to.
- Eat regular meals and do not skip them.
