# Accuracy by source format (stratum)

Generated by `scripts/fax_stratum_report.py`.

Closes Liebovitz review item 3.3 and the LOF stratified-testing
request of 26 Aug 2026, to the extent a simulated stratum can.

## What this is

Each document below was run twice: once clean, once after simulated
OCR degradation. **The clean run is the reference, not ground truth** -
these documents are not clinician-annotated, so this measures LOSS
under degradation rather than absolute accuracy.

The degraded set simulates scanner damage on text that was already
extracted cleanly. Real faxes also bring skew, speckle, dropped lines
and merged columns. **Every figure here is therefore a lower bound on
what a real fax would cost, and must not be described as a
measurement of real scanned documents.**

## How an apparent loss is classified

A first pass at this counted every value missing from a field as a
dropped one, and reported 40% of secondary diagnoses lost. That was
wrong. Checking where those values went showed the degraded run had
folded them INTO the primary diagnosis string - the extraction
behaved differently, it did not discard clinical content. Reporting
that as omission would have been a far more serious claim than the
evidence supports, so the three cases are now separated:

- **Absent** - gone from the extraction entirely. The real omission,
  and the only column that should worry anyone.
- **Relocated** - the text appears in another field.
- **Reworded** - the field kept its count; one phrasing replaced
  another.

## The safety-critical finding

**Warning signs retain 77.8% under degradation - 8 of 36 absent.** On clean
dictated documents the comparable figure is 93.7%. This is the
field where loss is most expensive, and it is the field that
degrades most.

The worst single case is `mtsamples_034`, which went from five
extracted warning signs to **zero**: chest pain, difficulty
breathing, shortness of breath, hemoptysis, and pain radiating
from the neck down the arm.

**Two things stop that being as bad as it sounds, and one thing
stops it being as good as it sounds.**

Agent 5 still produced a complete three-tier guide on the
degraded run, because its universal tier criteria do not depend
on extraction. A patient reading it is still told to call 911 if
they cannot catch their breath or have chest pain that will not
stop. The generic safety net held.

The extraction also RAISED warnings about its own degradation -
"Document uses abbreviated clinical shorthand", plus three
medications it could not confirm against the text. The system
knew something was wrong and said so.

But the DOCUMENT-SPECIFIC signs are gone with no trace.
**Hemoptysis - coughing up blood - appears nowhere in anything
the patient sees on the degraded run**, and neither does the
radiating neck-and-arm pain. Both are in the clean run. Those
are the two signs this patient's own team singled out, and they
are exactly what a universal tier list cannot replace.

So the honest statement is not "warning signs are lost" and not
"the safety net holds". It is: **under degradation the patient
keeps generic emergency advice and loses the specific warnings
written for them.**
